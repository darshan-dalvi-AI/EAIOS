  DYD  — Neha Bhosale (Delivery Manager), Sneha Kulkarni (Head of Managed Services),

IYENGAR: Let me be direct, because we've had this conversation before and nothing
has changed. We are on a Silver tier, I understand that. But ten of our fourteen
incidents this period missed the resolution target. That's seventy-one percent. At
what point does that stop being an SLA and start being a description of what you
can't do?

BHOSALE: That number is right and I'm not going to argue with it. Ten of fourteen.

IYENGAR: So what is the number supposed to be?

KULKARNI: Under Silver, the resolution targets are one hour for SEV-1, four hours
for SEV-2, eight for SEV-3. We are meeting those on our other accounts. We're not
meeting them on yours, and I want to explain why rather than apologise again.

IYENGAR: Please.

KULKARNI: Two things. First, five of your breaches trace to a single defect — a
memory leak in the order enrichment service. It's an unbounded cache with no
eviction. It has been in the backlog as NWR-1184 for three sprints. Every time it
comes up for planning it loses to feature work, because until the pod dies it
isn't visible to anyone.

KAUR: It's visible to us. It's visible at two in the morning.

KULKARNI: Yes. That's the point I'm conceding.

IYENGAR: And the second thing?

KULKARNI: Silver is an 08:00 to 22:00 support window, Monday to Saturday. Four of
your fourteen incidents opened outside it. We responded when the window opened,
which is contractually correct and operationally useless if your warehouses run
overnight.

IYENGAR: Our warehouses do run overnight. You know that.

BHOSALE: We do. The tier was set at contract signature in October, when the
overnight shift wasn't in scope. It became in scope in February and nobody went
back to the tier.

IYENGAR: So what do you owe us?

DESHPANDE: Under the master agreement, Silver carries a two percent service credit
per breached target, capped at ten percent of the monthly fee. Ten breaches is
twenty percent on the raw calculation. The cap brings it to ten percent. On a
nine lakh eighty thousand monthly fee, that's ninety-eight thousand rupees.

IYENGAR: Ninety-eight thousand. For seventy-one percent failure.

DESHPANDE: Yes. And I'd rather you heard the uncomfortable part from me: the cap
means breaches eleven through fourteen cost us nothing. The commercial mechanism
stopped applying pressure four incidents ago. That is a bad design and it's ours.

KAUR: I appreciate you saying it.

IYENGAR: What I actually want is not the credit. What is the plan?

DESHPANDE: Three things, with names and dates.

One — NWR-1184 gets fixed. Not prioritised, fixed. Kabir Menon takes it, it goes
into the sprint starting the twenty-eighth, and it is the first item, not the
last. Target in production by the fourteenth of August.

Two — we move you from Silver to Gold at DYD's cost for six months. That's 24×7
coverage and a thirty-minute first response instead of sixty. If at the end of six
months the numbers justify it, we talk commercially. If they don't, you drop back
and you've lost nothing.

IYENGAR: At your cost.

DESHPANDE: At our cost. The tier was wrong for your operation and we should have
caught it in February.

Three — a weekly SLA report to Ms. Kaur every Monday, showing every incident,
target, actual, and whether it breached. Not a summary. The raw list.

KAUR: I'd want the root causes too.

DESHPANDE: You'll have them.

IYENGAR: And the warehouse integration project? P09. That was meant to finish in
July.

BHOSALE: It's over budget and it's late. Fifty-six lakh spent against a fifty-two
lakh budget, so we're carrying a negative margin on it. That's our problem, not
yours — you're fixed price. But the date is your problem and I don't have a
credible new one for you today.

IYENGAR: When will you?

BHOSALE: Friday. I'd rather give you a date I've tested than one that sounds good
on this call.

IYENGAR: Fine. Friday. And I want to be clear about where we are — we're not
threatening to leave. But the master agreement has a chronic-failure clause, three
months at the cap, and we're one month in. I'd rather never find out whether we'd
use it.

DESHPANDE: Understood. So would I.
